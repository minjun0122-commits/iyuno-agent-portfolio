The NIST Cybersecurity Framework organizes security practice into six
functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover. Govern
(added in version 2.0) covers how an organization sets risk-management
strategy, roles, and policy. Identify covers asset inventory and risk
assessment. Protect covers access control, awareness training, and data
security. Detect covers continuous monitoring and anomaly detection. Respond
covers incident response planning and communication. Recover covers restoring
capabilities and services after an incident. The framework is deliberately
outcome-based rather than prescriptive, so organizations map their existing
controls to these functions instead of adopting a single fixed control list.
It is widely used as a common vocabulary between technical teams and
executive leadership when discussing security maturity.
