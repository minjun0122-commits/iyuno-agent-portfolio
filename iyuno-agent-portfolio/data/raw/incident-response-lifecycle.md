A typical incident response lifecycle has four phases: preparation
(maintaining playbooks, contact lists, and tooling before anything happens),
detection and analysis (confirming an event is a genuine incident and scoping
its impact), containment, eradication, and recovery (isolating affected
systems, removing the root cause, and restoring service), and post-incident
activity (a blameless retrospective that produces concrete follow-up actions).
Clear severity definitions and a designated incident commander role reduce
confusion during a live incident. Communication templates prepared in advance,
for both internal stakeholders and, where required, affected customers or
regulators, shorten response time when minutes matter.
